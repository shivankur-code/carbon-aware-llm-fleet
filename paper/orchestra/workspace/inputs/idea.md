## Problem Statement

Cloud operators can lower the carbon footprint of servers by building new server SKUs from energy-efficient CPUs together with *reused* components harvested from retired servers — DRAM attached over CXL and SSDs. Prior evaluations of such reuse-based designs assess a server design at a single point in time and under an average grid carbon intensity. Operators, however, make *sequential* decisions over a multi-year horizon: each quarter they decide whether to buy new hardware, build reused-component hosts, keep, cascade older servers to lighter service tiers (possibly in another region), lease capacity, or retire hardware. Reused parts are, by construction, already aged; DRAM and SSDs follow age-dependent failure curves, and every failure consumes a spare part (with its own embodied carbon), incurs repair logistics, and drains node capacity for hours to days, which must be covered by headroom that itself carries embodied and idle operational carbon. For large-language-model (LLM) inference fleets built on 8-GPU hosts, operational carbon is also much larger relative to embodied carbon than for CPU servers.

The problem: over a multi-year horizon, for an LLM-inference fleet spanning grids of different carbon intensity, (a) how much of the lifecycle-carbon saving of component reuse survives once age-dependent failures, spare parts, repair drains and service-level-objective (SLO) headroom are counted, (b) when does cascading older GPU servers to a batch-inference tier save carbon, and (c) what does guaranteeing a latency-critical SLO under correlated failures cost in carbon, with and without reuse?

## Core Hypothesis

1. Reuse savings for GPU inference hosts are strongly grid-dependent, because operational carbon dominates the lifecycle of such hosts; reuse matters most on clean grids.
2. Age-dependent failures erode reuse savings mainly through planning failures: a plan that ignores reliability can lose most of the saving or become carbon-negative, while a plan that prices reliability in keeps most of it and degrades gracefully as reused parts get worse.
3. Cascading old GPUs to batch inference avoids only embodied carbon of new hosts, so it saves carbon only when the old GPU's batch work per joule approaches that of the new GPU; moving cascaded hardware to a clean-grid region amplifies the benefit when it exists.
4. Guaranteeing an SLO requires idle reserve capacity; because embodied carbon dominates on idle capacity, reused-component hosts are the cheapest way to hold reserve, so reuse should remain beneficial — possibly more so — under a guaranteed SLO.

## Proposed Methodology (Detailed Technical Approach)

**Fleet state.** Time is discretized into quarters $t = 0,\dots,T-1$ ($T = 24$, six years). The fleet is a set of cohorts indexed by SKU $s$, service tier $\tau \in \{\text{LC}, \text{BATCH}\}$ (latency-critical and batch inference), region $r$, and node age $a$ in quarters within the node's current life. The decision variable $n_{s\tau r a t} \ge 0$ counts nodes. Three SKUs are modeled: a new 8-GPU host with all-new components (`new_gpu`), a reuse-based host with new GPUs, an efficient CPU, reused DRAM over CXL and reused SSDs whose parts enter at age $a_0$ (`green_gpu`), and a previous-generation 8-GPU host cascaded from its first life (`old_gpu`).

**Flow conservation.** For every cohort,
$$ n_{s\tau r a t} = n_{s\tau r,a-1,t-1} - x^{\text{ret}}_{s\tau r a t} - \sum_{r'} x^{\text{casc}}_{s r r' a t}\,[\tau=\text{LC}] + \sum_{r''} x^{\text{casc}}_{s r'' r a t}\,[\tau=\text{BATCH}] + x^{\text{buy}}_{s\tau r t}\,[a=0], $$
with the initial fleet at $t=0$ and forced retirement at a maximum age. Cascading moves a node from the LC tier of region $r$ to the BATCH tier of region $r'$ (geographic cascading if $r'\neq r$, with shipping carbon and cost) and keeps its age.

**Reliability as precomputed parameters.** Each component class $c$ (GPU, CPU, DRAM DIMM, SSD) has a Weibull hazard $h_c(a) = \frac{k_c}{\eta_c}\left(\frac{a}{\eta_c}\right)^{k_c-1}$, calibrated so that the annual failure probability at a reference age equals an annualized failure rate. A reused part enters at age $a_0>0$. A failed part is replaced by a new spare, so each component slot is a delayed renewal process whose expected failures per quarter are computed numerically. Because node age is a cohort index, the expected failures $\lambda_{s a}$, expected drained hours $d_{s a}$, spare-part embodied carbon and logistics per node-quarter are precomputed, and the optimization stays linear. An optional proportional-hazards multiplier $m$ scales the hazard of reused parts beyond what their age predicts.

**Capacity and demand.** Served work $w_{s\tau r a t}$ (in node-equivalents of a fully busy new host) satisfies $w \le n \cdot \theta_{s\tau}\, u \,(1 - \beta_\tau\, \ell_{s a})$, where $\theta_{s\tau}$ is relative throughput (including a CXL slowdown for the reuse-based host), $u$ the utilization cap, $\ell_{s a}$ the availability loss from drains, and $\beta_\tau$ a headroom multiplier for the LC tier. LC demand is per region; BATCH demand is global. Unserved demand is leased (carbon and cost premium) or counted as shortfall.

**Reused-part harvest pools.** Building a reuse-based host consumes harvested DIMMs and SSDs from pools fed by external supply (retirements of a general-purpose fleet) plus a screening yield times first-life parts of hosts retired in the fleet.

**Objective.** Lifecycle carbon
$$ C = \sum \big(e^{\text{emb}}_{s a} n + e^{\text{strand}}_{s a} x^{\text{ret}}\big) + \sum \big(P^{\text{idle}}_s (1-\ell_{sa}) H\, n + \kappa_{s\tau}\, w\big)\,\text{PUE}\cdot\text{CI}_{r t} + \sum (q^{\text{spare}}_{sa} + q^{\text{log}}_{sa})\, n + C^{\text{lease}} + C^{\text{ship}}, $$
where embodied carbon is amortized linearly over the design life with the unamortized remainder charged on early retirement, operational carbon uses an idle-plus-dynamic power model, and grid carbon intensity $\text{CI}_{rt}$ declines over time per region. Cost and shortfall are alternative objectives; Pareto fronts are traced by $\varepsilon$-constraint. At fleet scale the linear-programming relaxation is used.

**Experiment ladder.** E0: no reuse (only new hardware), with and without failures. E1: reuse allowed, failures ignored; X = saving vs E0 without failures. E2a: E1's plan replayed with failures. E2b: reuse with a reliability-aware plan; Y = saving vs E0 with failures. E3/E3g: E2b plus in-place / geographic cascading; Z = additional saving.

**Chance-constrained SLO headroom.** The number of LC nodes simultaneously drained is modeled as the occupancy of an M/G/∞ queue: Poisson with mean $\sum N_i d_i / H$ for independent failures, or negative binomial with dispersion $k$ for correlated failures. An outer loop solves the plan, samples instantaneous LC capacity per region-quarter, adds the gap between demand and the $\varepsilon$-quantile of capacity to a per-region-quarter installed-capacity reserve $R_{rt}$ (constraint: installed LC capacity plus lease $\ge D_{rt} + R_{rt}$), and re-solves until every region-quarter satisfies $P(\text{capacity} < \text{demand}) \le \varepsilon$. Plans are validated by an independent replay.

**Uncertainty.** One-at-a-time sensitivity (tornado) on every parameter with a range; triangular fuzzy factors on failure rates, embodied carbon and CXL slowdown propagated by $\alpha$-cuts with the vertex method; sweeps of reused-part failure multiplier, old-GPU batch efficiency, LC headroom and utilization, and embodied-carbon accounting conventions.

**Measurement methodology (prepared, not yet executed).** A serving benchmark measures, per GPU generation, LC goodput at a time-to-first-token and time-per-output-token SLO and BATCH throughput, with board power sampled to obtain tokens per joule; a KV-cache offload benchmark measures decoding throughput with the KV cache in local versus remote-NUMA host memory as a proxy for CXL-attached reused DRAM; a fault-injection study flips bits in the KV cache at controlled bit-error rates under no protection, SECDED ECC, and block checksums with recomputation. These measurements will replace placeholder throughput, power and CXL-slowdown parameters.

## Expected Contribution

1. The first reliability-aware, multi-period lifecycle optimization of reuse-based servers for LLM-inference fleets, coupling age-dependent component failures, spares and repair drains, reused-part supply, cascading (including geographic cascading), and regional grid decarbonization.
2. Quantitative break-even conditions: the reused-part failure-rate multiplier at which reuse stops paying for failure-blind versus reliability-aware planning, and the old-GPU batch energy efficiency at which cascading starts to pay.
3. A chance-constrained headroom method with Monte Carlo in the loop that guarantees per-region-quarter LC SLOs under correlated failures, and the finding that reuse remains beneficial under such guarantees because reused hardware is the cheapest idle reserve.
4. Design guidance for reliability-aware reuse-based AI servers: reuse-based hosts for reserve and lightly loaded capacity, most energy-efficient hosts for steadily loaded capacity, and grid-aware placement of reuse.
